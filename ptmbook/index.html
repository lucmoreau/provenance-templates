Provenance Engineering

The Provenance Template Management Approach

Luc Moreau — University of Sussex

From supply chains to AI: tracing what matters. Where did this come from? Who made it? Can we trust it?

About the book

Provenance is the systematic record of how data, goods, and decisions come to be. This book is an engineering-focused guide to building provenance systems that work in the real world. At its core is the concept of provenance templates: a declarative, design-first methodology that separates what provenance should be generated from how it is produced, with tooling automating instantiation, storage, querying, visualisation, and explanation at runtime. Every concept is underpinned by ProvToolbox, and all examples, code, and screenshots in the book are taken from the software.

Online material

  • Listings and their sources — every listing of the book, with the full files it was extracted from (PROV-N, JSON-LD, JSON schemas, generated Java, JavaScript, Python, and Rust)
  • The provenance template library — the foundational templates of Chapter 9, each at its own IRI
  • provenance-templates on GitHub — the template library, its configurations, and this site
  • ProvToolbox on GitHub — the toolkit: serialisations, validation, template instantiation, compiler, services
  • The PROV translator service — convert PROV documents between PROV-N, PROV-JSONLD, PROV-XML, and SVG

What readers will find

  • The W3C PROV standard: entities, activities, agents, and their interrelations.
  • A provenance-first design methodology, placing provenance at the core of software design.
  • Template management: authoring, instantiation, storing, and access control.
  • Visualising, querying, and reasoning over provenance for audit, compliance, and investigation.
  • Provenance template management with Retrieval-Augmented Generation, towards conversational agents.
  • A library of foundational templates ready to be customised and deployed.
  • A manifesto for provenance template management.

Who it is for

Software engineers building systems that must be auditable, compliant, or explainable; data scientists and AI practitioners grounding outputs in traceable evidence; supply-chain and compliance professionals; researchers in data-intensive fields requiring reproducibility; and graduate students entering provenance, knowledge representation, or semantic-web technologies.

This document specifies the two algorithms of the provenance template approach. Instantiation takes a template and a set of bindings and produces a PROV document in which the variables are replaced by their values; it is made of a simple substitution for one graph element, and a full template instantiation that applies the simple substitution to every element of a template, once for every combination of the values of its name variables. Merge combines several templates, or several documents, into one. The document specifies the well-formedness of instantiated elements, the treatment of unbound variables, the generation of identifiers, the reserved tmpl: attributes, the enumeration of values, and the error situations.

This is an unofficial draft, part of the PROV-TEMPLATE family of documents [[PROV-TEMPLATE-OVERVIEW]]. It has no official standing of any kind and does not represent the consensus of any standards organisation.

The instantiation algorithm is that of the book Provenance Engineering [[PTM-BOOK]] (section 4.4), which simplifies the approach of [[PROV-TEMPLATE-TSE]]; merge follows its section 5.3.2. ProvToolbox [[PROVTOOLBOX]] implements both; lists where its current implementation departs from this specification.

The artefacts on which these algorithms operate — templates, variables, bindings, the tmpl: vocabulary — are defined in [[PROV-TEMPLATE]]. A template processor conforms to this specification if, for every template and set of bindings, its instantiation produces the document specified here, up to the choice of fresh identifiers, and if it signals the error situations of .

Introduction

Instantiation transforms a template containing variables into a provenance document, using the values of a set of bindings. The algorithm consists of two components:

  1. a simple substitution for one graph element, a node or a relation, with a simple mapping that gives each variable at most one value or one sequence of values;
  2. a full template instantiation, which, for each element of the template, enumerates the combinations of the values of its name variables, builds a simple mapping for each, and calls the simple substitution.

An element that the simple substitution leaves without identifier, or without the properties a relation requires, is not well-formed and is left out of the result. This is how instantiation erases nodes, relations and attributes, renames variables, specialises templates and makes elements optional [[PTM-BOOK]].

Preliminaries

Graph elements and well-formedness

A graph element is a node or a relation. Adopting the PROV-N notation [[PROV-N]], the nodes are:

  • entity(id, attrs): id compulsory;
  • activity(id, t1, t2, attrs): id compulsory;
  • agent(id, attrs): id compulsory.

The relations, and the condition under which each is well-formed, are:

RelationWell-formed if
wasDerivedFrom(id; e2, e1, a, g, u, attrs)id exists, or e2 and e1 exist
used(id; a, e, t, attrs)id exists, or a and e exist
wasGeneratedBy(id; e, a, t, attrs)id exists, or e and a exist
wasInvalidatedBy(id; e, a, t, attrs)id exists, or e and a exist
wasAttributedTo(id; e, ag, attrs)id exists, or e and ag exist
wasAssociatedWith(id; a, ag, pl, attrs)id exists, or a and ag exist, or a and pl exist
actedOnBehalfOf(id; ag2, ag1, a, attrs)id exists, or ag2 and ag1 exist
wasStartedBy(id; a2, e, a1, t, attrs)id exists, or a2 and e exist, or a2 and a1 exist
wasEndedBy(id; a2, e, a1, t, attrs)id exists, or a2 and e exist, or a2 and a1 exist
wasInformedBy(id; a2, a1, attrs)id exists, or a2 and a1 exist
provext:alternateOf(id; e1, e2, attrs)id exists, or e1 and e2 exist
provext:specializationOf(id; e1, e2, attrs)id exists, or e1 and e2 exist
provext:hadMember(id; c, e, attrs)id exists, or c and e exist

A node is well-formed if it has an identifier. A relation carrying the reserved attribute tmpl:ID may be given an identifier by the algorithm, and is regarded as well-formed when it is. Relations alternateOf, specializationOf and hadMember are those of the provext namespace [[PROV-JSONLD]], which gives them identifiers and attributes like the other relations. Elements that are not well-formed are not included in the result of an instantiation.

Since templates are instantiated and merged, some variables may be instantiated at one time and others at another, a later merge providing the missing parts. An element given an identifier can be merged with other elements with the same identifier; this is why a relation with an identifier is well-formed even when some of its properties are missing.

The PROV edge properties of a relation are the positions e, e1, e2, a, a1, a2, ag, ag1, ag2, pl, g, u and c, whose values are identifiers; t is a time. In PROV-JSONLD, for instance, the edge properties a, ag and pl of an association are its JSON properties activity, agent and plan; role and other attributes are not edge properties.

Values, variables and reserved keywords

  • A concrete value is a number, a boolean, a string, a string paired with a type, or an identifier. An identifier (or name) is a qualified name; it may be a variable, a qualified name in the var or vargen namespace [[PROV-TEMPLATE]].
  • A ground value is a value that is not a variable.
  • A gensym variable is a variable in the vargen namespace. When it is unbound, the algorithm generates a fresh unique identifier for it, in the uuid namespace (urn:uuid:), and records it, so that all its occurrences in one instantiation receive the same identifier.
  • An attribute-value pair consists of an identifier, the attribute, and a value. An attribute in the tmpl namespace is a reserved keyword; reserved keywords drive the behaviour of the algorithm.

The following meta-variables are used:

  • v denotes a variable;
  • n denotes a name bound to a variable;
  • i denotes an identifier freshly generated by the algorithm;
  • x, xx denote a ground value and a sequence of ground values.

Simple substitution for a graph element

Objective, inputs and outputs

Given a graph element, construct a new graph element in which each variable is replaced with the value, or values, given by a simple mapping, which maps each variable to a single concrete value, to a sequence of concrete values, or to null to indicate that no value is available.

InputsI, a graph element; B, a simple mapping; GV, the gensym bindings accumulated so far (possibly empty).
OutputsO, a new element copying the structure of I, or an empty result; GV', GV extended with the gensym bindings created.

Process

Let O be a partial copy of I containing only its type, its identifier and its PROV edge properties, without attribute-value pairs, and GV' a copy of GV. O and GV' are updated by the process; I, B and GV are not. Let val(v) denote GV'(v) if v is a gensym variable, and B(v) otherwise.

For each variable v in the identifier or a PROV edge property of O:

  1. if v is a node identifier, apply case 1;
  2. else if v is a relation identifier, apply case 2;
  3. else (v is the value of a PROV edge property), apply case 3.

For each attribute-value pair (attr, val) of I:

  1. if the attribute is a variable, (v, val), apply case 4;
  2. else if the value is a variable, (attr, v):
    1. if attr is not a reserved keyword, apply case 5.a;
    2. else:
      1. if val(v) is a ground value x, apply case 5.b.1;
      2. else if val(v) is a sequence of ground values xx, apply case 5.b.2;
      3. else if val(v) is a variable v2, apply case 5.b.3;
      4. else if val(v) is a sequence of variables vv2, apply case 5.b.4;
      5. else if val(v) is null, apply case 5.b.5;
      6. else error (5.b.6).
  3. else (neither the attribute nor the value is a variable), add (attr, val) to O.

If O is empty or not well-formed, return the empty result; else return O and GV'. Well-formedness is determined after all cases have been applied, so that tmpl:ID can provide a missing identifier.

Cases

Case 1: a variable as node identifier

A node must have an identifier: an unbound variable that is not a gensym variable suppresses the node; a binding that is not a single name is an error.

  • if v is a gensym variable and GV'(v) is null, generate a fresh identifier i, replace v in O by i, and extend GV' with (v, i);
  • else if v is a gensym variable and GV'(v) = n, replace v by n;
  • else if B(v) is null, return the empty result;
  • else if B(v) = n, a single name, replace v by n;
  • else error (1).

Illustration

entity(vargen:e, [prov:location = "London"]), GV'(e) null: entity(uuid:a994fc11-…, [prov:location = "London"]), and GV' extended with (e, uuid:a994fc11-…).

entity(var:e, [prov:location = "London"]), B(e) = ex:entity1: entity(ex:entity1, [prov:location = "London"]).

entity(var:e, [prov:location = "London"]), B(e) null: the empty result.

entity(var:e, [prov:location = "London"]), B(e) = 1: error (1), 1 is not an identifier.

Case 2: a variable as relation identifier

Relation identifiers are optional: an unbound variable that is not a gensym variable gives an anonymous relation.

  • if v is a gensym variable and GV'(v) is null, generate i, replace v by i, extend GV' with (v, i);
  • else if v is a gensym variable and GV'(v) = n, replace v by n;
  • else if B(v) is null, clear the identifier of O;
  • else if B(v) = n, replace v by n;
  • else error (2).

Illustration

wasAttributedTo(var:attr; ex:e, ex:ag), B(attr) null: wasAttributedTo(ex:e, ex:ag).

wasAttributedTo(vargen:attr; ex:e, ex:ag), GV'(attr) null: wasAttributedTo(uuid:a994fc11-…; ex:e, ex:ag).

Case 3: a variable as the value of a PROV edge property

The value of an edge property must be a name.

  • if v is a gensym variable and GV'(v) is null, generate i, replace v by i, extend GV' with (v, i);
  • else if v is a gensym variable and GV'(v) = n, replace v by n;
  • else if B(v) is null, omit the property from O (well-formedness is at risk);
  • else if B(v) = n, replace v by n;
  • else error (3).

Illustration

wasAssociatedWith(ex:a, var:ag, -), B(ag) = ex:ag1: wasAssociatedWith(ex:a, ex:ag1, -).

wasAssociatedWith(ex:a, var:ag, -), B(ag) null: wasAssociatedWith(ex:a, -, -), which is not well-formed (no identifier, no agent, no plan): the empty result.

wasAssociatedWith(ex:a, ex:ag, var:pl), B(pl) null: wasAssociatedWith(ex:a, ex:ag, -), still well-formed.

wasAssociatedWith(ex:a, var:ag, -), B(ag) = 10: error (3).

Case 4: a variable as attribute

The attribute-value pair is (v, val), and an identifier is expected for v.

  • if v is a gensym variable and GV'(v) is null, generate i, extend GV' with (v, i), add (i, val) to O;
  • else if v is a gensym variable and GV'(v) = n, add (n, val);
  • else if B(v) is null, add nothing;
  • else if B(v) = n, add (n, val);
  • else if B(v) = nn, a sequence of names:
    • if val is a ground value x2, or a variable v2 with B(v2) = x2, add (n, x2) for each n in nn;
    • else if val is a variable v2 with B(v2) = xx2 and |xx2| = |nn|, add the pairs (n, x2) for corresponding n in nn and x2 in xx2;
    • else if val is a variable v2 with B(v2) null, add nothing;
  • else error (4).

Illustration

entity(ex:e, [var:prop1 = 10, ex:prop2 = 14]), B(prop1) = ex:p1: entity(ex:e, [ex:p1 = 10, ex:prop2 = 14]).

entity(ex:e, [var:prop1 = 10, ex:prop2 = 14]), B(prop1) null: entity(ex:e, [ex:prop2 = 14]).

entity(ex:e, [var:prop1 = 10]), B(prop1) = [ex:p1, ex:p2]: entity(ex:e, [ex:p1 = 10, ex:p2 = 10]).

entity(ex:e, [var:prop1 = 'var:v1']), B(prop1) = [ex:p1, ex:p2], B(v1) = ["a", "b"]: entity(ex:e, [ex:p1 = "a", ex:p2 = "b"]).

Case 5.a: a variable as value of an ordinary attribute

The attribute-value pair is (attr, v), where attr is neither a variable nor a reserved keyword.

  • if v is a gensym variable and GV'(v) is null, generate i, extend GV' with (v, i), add (attr, i);
  • else if v is a gensym variable and GV'(v) = n, add (attr, n);
  • else if B(v) is null, add nothing;
  • else if B(v) = x, add (attr, x);
  • else if B(v) = xx, add (attr, x) for each x in xx;
  • else error (5.a).

Illustration

entity(ex:e, [ex:prop1 = 'var:v', ex:prop2 = 14]), B(v) = 10: entity(ex:e, [ex:prop1 = 10, ex:prop2 = 14]).

entity(ex:e, [ex:prop1 = 'var:v']), B(v) = [ex:p1, ex:p2]: entity(ex:e, [ex:prop1 = 'ex:p1', ex:prop1 = 'ex:p2']).

Cases 5.b: a variable as value of a reserved keyword

The attribute-value pair is (attr, v), where attr is a reserved keyword. The semantics of tmpl:if and tmpl:notif are complementary: tmpl:if keeps the element only if the variable is bound, tmpl:notif only if it is not. A variable bound to a sequence counts as bound.

Keyword 5.b.1: val(v) = x 5.b.2: val(v) = xx 5.b.5: val(v) null
tmpl:labeladd (prov:label, x), x an xsd:stringadd (prov:label, x) for each x in xxomit
tmpl:timeadd (prov:time, x), x an xsd:dateTimeignore: one time onlyomit
tmpl:startTimeadd (prov:startTime, x)ignoreomit
tmpl:endTimeadd (prov:endTime, x)ignoreomit

tmpl:notifreturn the empty resultreturn the empty resultomit, keep the element
tmpl:IDif x is a name, O a relation and O has no identifier, set its identifier to x; else ignorereturn the empty result: an identifier is a single nameomit

Case 5.b.3, val(v) = v2, a variable: add (attr, v2) to O. The indirection is preserved, leaving v2 to a later instantiation.

Case 5.b.4, val(v) = vv2, a sequence of variables: add (attr, v2) for each v2 in vv2. This case is not meaningful for tmpl:time, tmpl:ID and tmpl:if, which expect a single value.

Illustration

entity(ex:e, [tmpl:label = 'var:v']), B(v) = ["hello", "bye"]: entity(ex:e, [prov:label = "hello", prov:label = "bye"]).

activity(ex:a, [tmpl:startTime = 'var:v']), B(v) = two times: activity(ex:a), the times ignored.

entity(ex:e, [tmpl:if = 'var:v']), B(v) null: the empty result.

entity(ex:e, [tmpl:notif = 'var:v']), B(v) = 10: the empty result.

wasAttributedTo(ex:e, ex:ag, [tmpl:ID = 'var:v']), B(v) = ex:id1: wasAttributedTo(ex:id1; ex:e, ex:ag).

entity(ex:e, [tmpl:label = 'var:v']), B(v) = var:v2: entity(ex:e, [tmpl:label = 'var:v2']).

Considerations

  • Variables may be mapped to other variables (cases 5.b.3 and 5.b.4, and names in cases 1 to 5.a). This gives instantiation the capability to rename variables, and to derive templates from templates [[PROV-TEMPLATE]].
  • Cases 5.b handle the syntactically constrained PROV attributes, label and times, through their reserved keywords.
  • Several tmpl:ID attributes on one element make the result depend on their order, and should be avoided.

Full template instantiation

Objective, inputs and outputs

Given a template and a set of bindings, build an instantiated provenance document by applying the bindings to each element of the template. The bindings associate name variables, the variables used in identifier positions, with arrays of names, which generate multiple copies of each element by Cartesian product; and value variables, the variables used in attribute or value positions, with arrays of arrays of values, which supply the attribute values of each copy [[PROV-TEMPLATE]].

Inputsa template T, consisting of a bundle whose identifier is a variable β (regular or gensym); a set of bindings B, with its name variables NV and value variables VV; if β is a regular variable, B(β) is a single identifier.
Outputsa provenance document D; the bindings GV of the gensym variables created.

Process

Let GV be empty if β is regular, or {(β, i)}, with i a fresh identifier, if β is a gensym variable. Let D be a document with an empty bundle, identified by B(β) or GV(β).

For each element E of T:

  1. Let NVE be the sequence of the variables of NV in identifier position in E (its identifier and its PROV edge properties), in lexicographic order of their names, followed by the gensym variables in identifier position in E, in order of appearance.
  2. Let VVE be the set of the variables of VV in attribute or value position in E, with the gensym variables in value position in E.
  3. Let NVE = (v0, …, vk). If NVE is empty, the Cartesian product below contains exactly one element, the empty tuple, and t = 1.
  4. For each vj, its value array (xj,0, …, xj,lj) is the array of names B(vj) if vj is bound in B; the names GV(vj) if vj is a gensym variable already bound in GV; and (null), a one-element array, if vj is unbound or a gensym variable not yet in GV, so that it still contributes one slot to the product.
  5. Let t = (l0 + 1) × … × (lk + 1).
  6. Let CP be the Cartesian product of the value arrays, enumerated so that vk, the last variable, cycles fastest, and v0 slowest: element i, 0 ≤ i < t, is the tuple (y0, …, yk) with yj = xj, (i div stridej) mod (lj+1), where stridej = (lj+1 + 1) × … × (lk + 1) and stridek = 1.
  7. For each i, 0 ≤ i < t:
    • let M map (v0, …, vk) to CP(i);
    • let B1 extend M, for each vv in VVE, with (vv, b), where b is B(vv)(i) if B(vv) has length t; B(vv)(0) if it has length 1, a constant for all copies; null if vv is unbound; and error (10) for any other length;
    • apply the simple substitution to E, B1 and GV, giving E2 and GV2;
    • if E2 is not empty, add E2 to the bundle of D and replace GV by GV2.

Return D and GV.

The algorithm is deterministic, up to the choice of fresh identifiers, and terminates, since it iterates over the finite elements of T and the finite elements of each Cartesian product.

Linked variables

        

          The Cartesian product of step 6 can produce many copies of an element when several variables have many
          values. Variables may instead be linked [[PROV-TEMPLATE]], by the reserved keyword
          tmpl:linked in the template or by the member linked of the bindings: linked name
          variables MUST have value arrays of the same length, and are iterated in lock step rather than by Cartesian
          product. In step 6, a set of linked variables contributes a single dimension to the product, whose
          p-th element gives each of them its p-th value; the dimension is placed at the
          position of the first of the linked variables in NVE.
        

        

        

        
[[PTM-BOOK]] mentions this option, which it calls aligned variables, without specifying it; its integration into the enumeration above is to be confirmed.

      

    


    

      
Merge

      

        A provenance engineer may wish to enrich a template with new elements and attribute-value pairs, defined
        in a separate template; enriching the template is then merging it with the supplementary one
        [[PTM-BOOK]]. Merge applies equally to instantiated documents.
      

      

        PROV is inspired by the Semantic Web, in which descriptions form a graph: in a first approximation, the
        merge of two provenance graphs is the union of their RDF representations. Specifically:
      

      
    
        
  • nodes with the same identifier are merged into one node, which has all their attribute-value pairs;
    • 
        
  • relations with the same identifier are merged in the same manner;
    • 
        
  • the uniqueness and key constraints of PROV [[PROV-CONSTRAINTS]] apply, which require merging further
          relations, and from which a normal form of the merged graph is obtained;
    • 
        
  • since a template is a bundle, two templates are merged only if their bundles have the same identifier,
          which renaming by instantiation can ensure.
    • 
      

      

        Merge does not rename variables: a variable is a qualified name, so a variable that occurs in two templates
        denotes the same node in both. Depending on the identifiers they share, merging templates can therefore
        produce different topologies: two derivations, merged, form a fork, a join, a sequence, a parallel
        composition, a single derivation if they have the same identifier, or a loop.
      

      

        

        

      

      

      

        Merge can produce problematic graphs, by introducing cycles of derivations or by making a temporal
        interpretation of the graph impossible. Merging templates must be done with care, which may require
        renaming some variables before the merge.
      

      

        In a template transformation file [[PROV-TEMPLATE]], a merge task merges its inputs, in the order given.
      

    


    

      
Errors

      
A template processor MUST signal the following error situations.

      

        
ErrorSituation

        
          
(1)A node identifier variable is bound to a value that is not a single name (case 1).

          
(2)A relation identifier variable is bound to a value that is not a single name (case 2).

          
(3)A PROV edge property variable is bound to a value that is not a single name (case 3).

          
(4)An attribute variable is bound to a value that is neither a name nor a sequence of names, or the value sequence does not match it (case 4).

          
(5.a)The value variable of an ordinary attribute has an unexpected binding (case 5.a).

          
(5.b.6)The value variable of a reserved keyword has an unexpected binding (cases 5.b).

          
(10)A value variable is bound to an array whose length is neither 1 nor the number of copies t of its element (full instantiation, step 7).

        
      

      
A template processor SHOULD warn about variables of the template left unbound, and about variables of the bindings that the template does not use.

    


    

      
ProvToolbox and this specification

      
ProvToolbox (branch development2_0, 2026-10-16) currently departs from this specification as follows.

      
    
        
  • Enumeration order. ProvToolbox enumerates the Cartesian product with the first variable
          cycling fastest. Since value variables are indexed by position, the attribution of the illustration
          receives ex:t2a, ex:t2b on (ex:en1, ex:ag2) rather than on
          (ex:en2, ex:ag1).
    • 
        
  • Well-formedness. ProvToolbox keeps relations without identifier whose required properties are
          missing, such as used(ex:writing, -, -) in the second illustration; and it drops an
          attribution or association without agent even when it has an identifier.
    • 
        
  • tmpl:if and tmpl:notif are not implemented by the interpreter: the
          element is kept, and a bound tmpl:if remains in the output.
    • 
        
  • Times. A time variable bound to several values gives the last one, rather than being ignored.
    • 
        
  • Value variables of the wrong length reuse their first entry, with a warning, rather than raising
          error (10); a group variable bound to a literal leaves its position empty rather than raising errors (1)
          to (3).
    • 
        
  • Order attribute. On request (-genorder), ProvToolbox annotates each instantiated
          element with tmpl:order, the index of the combination that produced it.
    • 
        
  • Unsupported relations. wasInfluencedBy, mentionOf and the dictionary
          relations are dropped; hadMember with several members is not supported.
    • 
      

    


    

      
Relation to earlier work

      

        The templating system was first described in an unofficial draft of 2014 [[PROV-TEMPLATE-2014]] and in
        [[PROV-TEMPLATE-TSE]]. Their instantiation algorithm partitioned the variables of a template into groups of
        linked variables, combined the groups by Cartesian product, and annotated each instantiated element with
        tmpl:order. The algorithm of [[PTM-BOOK]] specified here simplifies it: it applies a case
        analysis per graph element, decides by well-formedness which elements are kept, specifies the reserved
        keywords tmpl:if, tmpl:notif and tmpl:ID, and leaves linked
        variables to an extension.
